## Welcome to My GitHub Garage

My journey into software development started in 2023 with a simple 9×9 Sudoku solver. At the time, I had no idea how many projects I'd end up building or how much I'd learn along the way.

Since then, I've worked on projects like **Docali Training, Chainbreakers, the SHPE UCF Discord Bot**, and plenty more. Some started as ideas I was excited to bring to life, while others came from problems I personally needed to solve.

One example is my **Image Resizer**. While preparing screenshots for my app's App Store Connect listing, I needed images with specific dimensions. Most online tools had upload limits, restrictions, or features locked behind paywalls. So I decided to build my own. That's something I love about programming: when I run into a problem, I have the ability to create my own solution.

Over time, I've also had opportunities to step into leadership roles. For my **Contact Manager** project, I served as Project Manager, leading a team of six developers: two focused on databases, two on APIs, and two on frontend development. It taught me a lot about communication, teamwork, accountability, and, perhaps most importantly, what to do differently next time.

More recently, I've been leading the same team in developing a **Group Expense Tracker**, a web and mobile application designed to make splitting expenses and paying people back easier. We're hoping to eventually launch the mobile app on the App Store.

One of my favorite parts of this experience has been working with teammates who may not have extensive development experience yet but have something just as important: **a willingness to learn**. I've been in their position before, and I want to be someone they can rely on for guidance, support, and encouragement as we figure things out together.

### Why I Call This My Garage

I like to think of my GitHub as my digital garage. It's where I experiment, build things, break things, fix them, and occasionally leave unfinished projects lying around.

Some repositories are polished applications. Others are small utilities, class assignments, experiments, or ideas that never quite made it past the prototype stage. My older repositories are admittedly a little scattered, and I plan to organize them with clearer names, descriptions, and documentation.

But that's also part of the journey. Each repository represents something I wanted to try, a problem I needed to solve, or a lesson I learned.

**This isn't just a collection of code. It's a record of my growth as a developer, teammate, and leader.**

Feel free to look around. There's always something being worked on in the garage.
